# Paper 1. The changing burden of disease in Nigeria and Western Sub-Saharan Africa, 1990–2023: trends in premature mortality, disability and health loss

**Author:** Ekemini Thompson  
**Study framework:** Global Burden of Disease Study 2023 (GBD 2023)  
**Article type:** Population-level secondary analysis manuscript framework

## Abstract

### Background
Nigeria faces a changing mix of communicable, maternal, neonatal and nutritional conditions, non-communicable diseases, and injuries. Understanding the balance between premature mortality and non-fatal health loss is essential for setting health-system priorities. The most recent GBD round provides a harmonised time series that can support assessment of these changes over 1990–2023.

### Aim
To characterise changes in disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs), years of life lost (YLLs), and years lived with disability (YLDs) in Nigeria from 1990 to 2023, and to place national patterns alongside the GBD Western Sub-Saharan Africa regional benchmark.

### Methods
This population-level secondary analysis is designed around GBD 2023 estimates for 1990–2023. Outcomes will be summarised as counts and rates, with crude and age-standardised rates kept distinct. Comparisons will use consistent location, year, age, sex, measure, metric, and release definitions. Nigeria will be compared descriptively with Western Sub-Saharan Africa, recognising that Nigeria is included in that regional aggregate. Temporal change will be summarised using endpoint differences, relative percentage change, and annualised percentage change. Age- and sex-specific patterns will be described. Where compatible age-specific population and rate inputs are available, count changes will be decomposed into population growth, age-structure change, and rate change. GBD uncertainty intervals will be reported, and paired draws will be retained for derived contrasts when available.

### Contribution
The analysis is designed to update the established Nigeria–West Africa disease-burden evidence base using GBD 2023, extend the observation period through 2023, and distinguish changes in total health loss from changes in age-standardised rates.

**Keywords:** Nigeria; West Africa; Global Burden of Disease; DALYs; YLLs; YLDs; premature mortality; disability; age-standardised rates; uncertainty.

## 1. Introduction

Population health is shaped by the interaction of demographic change, epidemiological transitions, environmental exposures, health-system performance, and social conditions. In Nigeria, these forces operate across a large and diverse population and produce simultaneous demands from infectious diseases, maternal and neonatal conditions, injuries, and chronic non-communicable diseases. A national burden profile therefore needs to account for both fatal and non-fatal outcomes rather than relying on mortality alone.

The disability-adjusted life-year provides a common measure of health loss by combining years of life lost through premature death with years lived with disability. Its components are analytically important: YLLs capture fatal burden, whereas YLDs describe non-fatal health loss. Changes in total counts may reflect population growth and shifts in age structure as well as changes in age-specific disease rates. Age-standardised rates provide a complementary view by reducing the influence of differences in population age structure.

A previous systematic analysis compared Nigeria with 15 other West African countries using GBD 2019 and documented substantial improvements in life expectancy alongside persistent health-system and disease-burden challenges (Angell et al., 2022). GBD 2023 supersedes earlier GBD estimates and provides a harmonised 1990–2023 series across 204 countries and territories, with estimates for 375 diseases and injuries and uncertainty quantified from 250 draws (GBD 2023 Collaborators, 2025). An updated analysis can therefore assess whether the balance of premature mortality and disability has changed over the additional years and provide a contemporary reference for policy discussion.

The regional comparator requires careful interpretation. Nigeria is part of the GBD-defined Western Sub-Saharan Africa region. The regional estimate is consequently a nested benchmark rather than an independent comparison population. This paper will preserve that distinction and avoid presenting the regional aggregate as a control group.

## 2. Objectives

The primary objective is to describe the evolution of disease burden in Nigeria between 1990 and 2023 using DALYs, YLLs, and YLDs.

Specific objectives are to:

1. quantify endpoint and temporal changes in total DALYs, YLLs, and YLDs;
2. report counts alongside crude and age-standardised rates;
3. describe the contribution of leading causes to fatal and non-fatal health loss;
4. compare Nigeria's profile descriptively with the GBD Western Sub-Saharan Africa benchmark;
5. characterise age- and sex-specific patterns; and
6. separate demographic contributions to changes in counts where the required compatible inputs are available.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Study design and data source

This is a descriptive population-level secondary analysis of GBD 2023 estimates. The study period is 1990–2023. GBD estimates are modelled population-level outputs and are not individual-level observations or a new independent re-estimation of disease burden. The GBD 2023 study describes the estimation framework, data sources, cause hierarchy, and uncertainty procedures (GBD 2023 Collaborators, 2025).

### 3.2 Locations and comparator

Nigeria is the primary location. Western Sub-Saharan Africa is the regional benchmark. Because Nigeria is included in this regional aggregate, the comparison will be interpreted as a national estimate positioned against its encompassing regional context. A leave-Nigeria-out regional estimate is a distinct secondary estimand and requires verified GBD 2023 country membership and compatible country counts and population denominators, or paired draws. Country rates will not be averaged without population weighting.

### 3.3 Outcomes and measures

The principal outcomes are:

- **DALYs:** the sum of YLLs and YLDs;
- **YLLs:** years of life lost due to premature mortality; and
- **YLDs:** years lived in less than full health.

Counts and rates will be presented separately. Crude rates and age-standardised rates will not be treated as interchangeable. Age-standardised rates will use the GBD 2023 standard population and source-provided definitions. Deaths, prevalence, and incidence may be used as secondary descriptors when they clarify the interpretation of the principal outcomes.

### 3.4 Stratification and cause ranking

The headline series will cover both sexes and all ages, with sex- and age-stratified descriptions where supported by the source. Leading causes will be ranked separately by DALY counts and age-standardised DALY rates. Cause hierarchy levels will be respected: parent and child categories will not be added together. All comparisons will retain consistent GBD release, measure, metric, age group, sex, location, and year definitions.

### 3.5 Statistical analysis

The primary temporal contrast is 1990 versus 2023. For each outcome, the analysis will calculate:

- absolute change: 2023 estimate minus 1990 estimate;
- relative percentage change: (2023 / 1990 − 1) × 100; and
- annualised percentage change: ((2023 / 1990)^(1/33) − 1) × 100, where both endpoint estimates are positive.

Annual time series will be plotted to describe trajectories. Exploratory log-linear trend summaries may be used where appropriate; these will not be described as joinpoint regression and will not be interpreted causally.

Where age-specific population and rate inputs are available for both endpoints, decomposition will partition changes in counts into population growth, age-structure change, and age-specific rate change. This is an accounting decomposition, not causal attribution.

### 3.6 Uncertainty

GBD 2023 uncertainty intervals will be reported as 95% uncertainty intervals rather than conventional confidence intervals. The GBD 2023 estimates use 250 draws, with uncertainty intervals defined by the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the draw distribution. Where paired draws are available and permitted, derived changes will be calculated draw-by-draw while preserving draw identifiers. Joint uncertainty will not be reconstructed from marginal interval limits.

### 3.7 Data quality and reproducibility

Each extract will be documented by GBD release, location and cause identifiers, year range, age, sex, measure, metric, unit, age standard, extraction date, source filename, and checksum. Validation will assess missing fields, duplicate estimate keys, invalid intervals, inconsistent units, and hierarchy overlap. Analysis scripts and configuration will be version controlled.

### 3.8 Reporting

Reporting will follow the GATHER recommendations for health estimates and applicable STROBE principles for observational analyses. The study will distinguish modelled estimates from directly observed data and will document uncertainty, data provenance, and methodological comparability.

## 4. Interpretation framework

The analysis will interpret changes in counts and age-standardised rates jointly. Rising counts alongside falling age-standardised rates would indicate that demographic expansion may be contributing to the total number of health-loss years even as age-specific or age-standardised burden improves. Conversely, rising age-standardised rates would indicate that changes extend beyond population size and age structure. YLL and YLD trends will be considered separately to avoid obscuring shifts between premature mortality and disability.

The Western Sub-Saharan Africa comparison will be treated as contextual benchmarking, not an independent causal or statistical control. Differences will not be attributed to health-system performance without additional evidence.

## 5. Strengths and limitations

The principal strengths are the use of a consistent GBD 2023 time series, simultaneous assessment of fatal and non-fatal burden, explicit separation of counts and standardised rates, and transparent treatment of regional nesting and uncertainty. The analysis also provides a reproducible structure for ranking causes and describing demographic contributions.

Interpretation is subject to the limitations of modelled GBD estimates, including variation in the availability and quality of primary data across locations and years, uncertainty in cause assignment and modelling, and changes in estimation methods between GBD rounds. The analysis is descriptive and cannot establish causal explanations for temporal patterns.

## 6. Public-health relevance

A joint account of DALYs, YLLs, and YLDs can support prioritisation across prevention, treatment, rehabilitation, and health-system planning. Separating fatal from non-fatal burden helps avoid a policy focus based solely on deaths, while age-standardised rates help distinguish epidemiological change from demographic expansion. The national and regional profiles can provide a common evidence base for setting priorities and identifying areas that merit more detailed investigation.
