# Paper 2. Neurological disorders and vision loss in Nigeria and Western Sub-Saharan Africa, 1990–2023: a focused analysis of mortality, disability and uncertainty

**Author:** Ekemini Thompson  
**Study framework:** Global Burden of Disease Study 2023 (GBD 2023)  
**Article type:** Population-level secondary analysis manuscript framework

## Abstract

### Background
Neurological disorders and vision loss contribute to health loss through premature mortality, long-term disability, and limitations in daily functioning. Their burden spans multiple age groups and cause categories, making careful definition of the estimand essential when comparing conditions over time and across locations.

### Aim
To describe the burden and temporal patterns of neurological causes and blindness and vision loss in Nigeria from 1990 to 2023, using Western Sub-Saharan Africa as a nested regional benchmark.

### Methods
This population-level secondary analysis is structured around GBD 2023 estimates for 1990–2023. The primary neurological estimand is the basic GBD Level 2 neurological cause group. A separate expanded nervous-system estimand will be defined using a prespecified cross-hierarchy condition list and sequela-level estimates where needed to isolate neurological health loss. Blindness and vision loss will be analysed as an impairment outcome, separately from ophthalmic causes such as cataract, refractive disorders, glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy, and age-related macular degeneration. DALYs, YLLs, and YLDs will be summarised as counts and age-standardised rates, stratified by age and sex where supported. Endpoint changes and uncertainty will be calculated using source-provided intervals and paired draws where available. Nigeria will be compared descriptively with the Western Sub-Saharan Africa aggregate, which includes Nigeria.

### Contribution
The analysis separates a standard GBD neurological cause group from a broader nervous-system health-loss construct and distinguishes vision impairment from the causes that produce it. These distinctions support transparent, non-overlapping estimates and clearer interpretation of neurological and vision-related burden.

**Keywords:** neurological disorders; nervous system; blindness; vision loss; Nigeria; West Africa; DALYs; YLDs; YLLs; GBD 2023.

## 1. Introduction

Neurological conditions and vision loss are important sources of mortality and disability across the life course. Their effects range from acute fatal events and childhood-onset conditions to chronic disorders that contribute to long-term functional limitation. The burden is not fully represented by mortality statistics: many conditions contribute substantially through years lived with disability, while others cause premature death and prolonged neurological sequelae.

The GBD 2021 nervous-system analysis expanded earlier neurological groupings to include 37 conditions and selected neurological consequences of conditions classified elsewhere in the cause hierarchy. It used sequela-level estimation to isolate health loss directly attributable to nervous-system damage, including selected congenital, neonatal, infectious, and metabolic conditions (GBD 2021 Nervous System Disorders Collaborators, 2024). This approach demonstrates why a broad nervous-system estimand cannot be reproduced simply by summing all diagnoses that may have neurological manifestations.

Vision loss presents a related classification challenge. Blindness and vision loss are impairment outcomes, whereas cataract, refractive disorders, glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy, and age-related macular degeneration are causes. The impairment estimate and cause-specific estimates answer different questions and may overlap. They should therefore be reported in separate analytical streams rather than added together. Previous GBD-based work has described global and regional trends in blindness and vision loss through 2021 (Que et al., 2025).

Nigeria's disease-burden profile has been compared with other West African countries using GBD 2019, providing a regional context for updated analyses (Angell et al., 2022). Extending the analysis to GBD 2023 creates an opportunity to examine neurological and vision-related health loss over 1990–2023 while applying explicit definitions to avoid hierarchy overlap and double counting.

## 2. Objectives

The primary objective is to characterise neurological and vision-related health loss in Nigeria between 1990 and 2023.

Specific objectives are to:

1. describe DALYs, YLLs, and YLDs for the basic GBD Level 2 neurological cause group;
2. describe a separately defined expanded nervous-system estimand where release-specific cause and sequela data support it;
3. quantify blindness and vision loss as an impairment outcome;
4. describe selected ophthalmic causes separately from the impairment outcome;
5. compare counts and age-standardised rates by year, age, and sex; and
6. position Nigeria's estimates alongside the Western Sub-Saharan Africa regional benchmark.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Study design and source

This is a descriptive population-level secondary analysis of GBD 2023 estimates for 1990–2023. The GBD cause and impairment hierarchies will be treated as release-specific classification systems. The analysis will not interpret modelled estimates as individual-level observations or as direct clinical prevalence measurements.

### 3.2 Locations and regional context

Nigeria is the primary location. Western Sub-Saharan Africa is the regional benchmark. Nigeria is included in the regional aggregate; comparisons will therefore be descriptive and explicitly non-independent. A leave-Nigeria-out regional aggregate will only be considered if exact country membership and compatible counts, denominators, or paired draws can be verified.

### 3.3 Neurological estimands

Two neurological estimands will be kept distinct.

**Primary estimand: basic GBD Level 2 neurological cause group.** This is the standard cause-hierarchy group and will be extracted using the GBD 2023 cause identifiers and hierarchy metadata.

**Secondary estimand: expanded nervous-system health loss.** This is a broader, prespecified cross-hierarchy construct informed by the GBD 2021 nervous-system framework. It may include selected conditions classified outside the basic neurological cause group, such as stroke, meningitis, encephalitis, selected neonatal and congenital conditions, neurological sequelae of infectious diseases, diabetic neuropathy, and nervous-system cancers. Inclusion will depend on GBD 2023 availability and the ability to identify the relevant neurological health-loss component. Where a condition's neurological sequelae can be isolated, the analysis will use sequela-level estimates rather than the total burden of the parent condition.

The expanded estimand will preserve cause identifiers, hierarchy levels, and inclusion rationale. Parent and child causes will not be summed together. Conditions will not be included merely because they can have neurological symptoms; the analysis requires a defined nervous-system health-loss component.

### 3.4 Vision estimands

**Impairment outcome:** blindness and vision loss will be analysed as an impairment outcome, using the relevant GBD 2023 impairment definition and identifiers.

**Cause-specific outcomes:** cataract, refractive disorders, glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy, age-related macular degeneration, and other separately available ophthalmic causes will be analysed as causes.

The impairment estimate will not be added to ophthalmic cause estimates. Cause-specific analyses will be interpreted as attribution by underlying cause, while the impairment analysis will describe the overall vision-related health-loss outcome. The two streams will be displayed separately in tables and figures.

### 3.5 Outcomes and stratification

The main outcomes are DALYs, YLLs, and YLDs. For vision impairment, prevalence and YLDs will be important supplementary outcomes where available. Estimates will be reported as counts and age-standardised rates, with crude rates presented separately where useful. Both-sex totals will provide the headline series; age- and sex-stratified estimates will be examined where supported by the source.

### 3.6 Statistical analysis

The principal contrast is 1990 versus 2023. Absolute and relative changes and annualised percentage changes will be calculated using the same definitions across all outcomes. Annual series will be plotted to show temporal patterns. Cause rankings will be reported separately for counts and age-standardised rates. Neurological and vision outcomes will not be pooled into a single composite total.

Where the expanded nervous-system estimand includes multiple conditions, the analysis will check for overlap at the cause and sequela level before aggregation. The same rule will apply to ophthalmic cause categories. If mutually exclusive aggregation cannot be established, component estimates will be reported individually rather than summed.

### 3.7 Uncertainty

GBD 2023 95% uncertainty intervals will be reported as uncertainty intervals. Where draw-level data are available and permitted, derived changes and aggregate estimates will be calculated using paired draws while preserving the original draw identifiers. Marginal interval limits will not be added or used to infer joint uncertainty. If paired draws are unavailable, the analysis will report source-provided marginal uncertainty and avoid unsupported claims about uncertainty in differences.

### 3.8 Data validation and reproducibility

For each neurological and vision extract, the analysis will record GBD release, location, cause or impairment identifier, hierarchy level, year, age, sex, measure, metric, unit, and uncertainty fields. Validation will include duplicate-key checks, interval ordering, non-negative estimates, year coverage, hierarchy consistency, and overlap review. The crosswalk for the expanded nervous-system estimand will be version controlled and accompanied by an inclusion rationale for every condition.

### 3.9 Reporting

The manuscript will follow GATHER guidance and applicable STROBE principles. Tables and figures will clearly label the neurological estimand, vision impairment outcome, and ophthalmic cause analyses. The reporting will distinguish standard GBD cause categories from the expanded cross-hierarchy construct.

## 4. Interpretation framework

The analysis will distinguish fatal neurological burden from non-fatal disability and will interpret age-specific patterns in the context of conditions that affect different stages of life. The expanded nervous-system estimand will be discussed as a broader construct than the standard GBD Level 2 cause group, not as a replacement for it.

For vision, impairment estimates will be interpreted as the overall health loss associated with blindness and vision loss, while ophthalmic cause estimates will be interpreted as cause-specific contributions. Their values will not be combined. Counts and age-standardised rates will be considered together to distinguish demographic growth from changes in standardised burden.

The regional estimate will remain a nested benchmark because Nigeria is included in Western Sub-Saharan Africa. Descriptive differences will not be treated as evidence of causal effects or as independent statistical comparisons.

## 5. Strengths and limitations

The principal strengths are the explicit separation of standard and expanded neurological estimands, the use of release-specific identifiers and hierarchy metadata, the distinction between vision impairment and ophthalmic causes, and the planned use of paired draws for derived uncertainty where available. The life-course and age/sex structure of the analysis can also help identify how neurological and vision-related health loss is distributed across populations.

The analysis inherits limitations of modelled GBD estimates, including uneven source-data availability, uncertainty in cause and sequela estimation, and possible changes in estimation methods between GBD rounds. The expanded nervous-system grouping is an analytical construct and must remain transparent about its inclusion criteria. It should not be interpreted as a single native GBD cause category.

## 6. Public-health relevance

Neurological health loss requires coordinated prevention, acute care, long-term treatment, rehabilitation, and support for people living with disability. Vision-related health loss requires a complementary focus on prevention, early detection, treatment, and access to eye-care services. By separating impairment outcomes from underlying causes, the analysis can inform both population-level planning and cause-specific service priorities without double counting.
